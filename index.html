The side income you forgot to pre-tax

A $500 freelance project is really a $423.50 project. Self-employment tax takes 15.3 percent of net side income, which is $76.50 on that invoice, and it gets its turn before income tax does. The hourly rate you mentally quoted for the job never existed. The receipts-first habit that separates a real hourly rate from a fantasy one starts by carving the tax line out of every payment the day it lands.

Scale it and the number gets loud. Side income of $2,000 net carries a $306 self-employment tax bill, which at a $30 an hour target is 10.2 hours of the year worked just to hand the tax bill its share. Hold the set-aside the day each deposit clears: $76.50 per $500 invoice, $15.30 per $100, and the quarterly bill never ambushes the checking account.

The calendar matters as much as the rate. Estimated taxes come due four times a year, and the safe rule is to pay either 90 percent of this year's bill or 100 percent of last year's, whichever you can document; underpay and the penalty compounds each quarter. Below $400 of net self-employment income for the whole year the tax does not apply at all, which makes $400 the honest floor when you are deciding whether a hustle's margins can survive the paperwork.

The gig app already took its cut and the bank already took its float. The tax line is the one cost with a deadline attached. The side hustle math that survives it, price by price, is what ExtraHustles exists to run.
